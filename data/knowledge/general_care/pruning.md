# Pruning for Disease Prevention

## Why Pruning Matters for Plant Health
Proper pruning improves air circulation, reduces humidity within the canopy, removes diseased tissue, and promotes overall plant vigor. For many plant diseases — especially fungal diseases that require moisture for infection — an open, well-pruned canopy is one of the best defenses.

## General Pruning Principles
1. **Remove dead, diseased, or damaged wood first** — these are called the "3 D's."
2. **Cut to promote air flow** — thin interior branches to allow sunlight and air to penetrate.
3. **Make clean cuts** — ragged or torn cuts heal slowly and invite infection. Use sharp, clean tools.
4. **Prune at the right time** — timing varies by crop but is critical for disease management.

## Pruning Guidelines by Crop

### Apple and Stone Fruit Trees
- **When**: Dormant season (late winter, before bud break) for most structural pruning.
- **Remove**: Dead wood, crossing branches, water sprouts, and cankered limbs.
- **Open center**: Train to an open-center or modified central leader form for maximum air circulation.
- **Disease removal**: Cut well below visible cankers or lesions (at least 6-12 inches into healthy wood). Disinfect tools between cuts on diseased wood.
- **Avoid**: Pruning during wet weather, which spreads bacterial and fungal pathogens.

### Grape Vines
- **When**: Late dormant season (late winter to early spring).
- **Remove**: All mummified fruit (black rot), cankered or discolored canes, and excessive growth.
- **Canopy management**: Summer hedging, shoot positioning, and leaf removal around fruit clusters reduce humidity and improve air flow.
- **Esca/trunk disease**: Make pruning cuts during dry weather; protect large wounds with wound sealant or *Trichoderma* paste.

### Tomatoes
- **Indeterminate varieties**: Remove suckers below the first flower cluster. Prune to 1-3 main stems.
- **Lower leaves**: Remove leaves below the lowest fruit cluster to reduce soil-splash infections (early blight, septoria).
- **Staking/caging**: Keep plants off the ground.
- **When**: Prune in dry conditions; avoid wet foliage to prevent spreading bacterial diseases.

### Berry Crops (Strawberry, Raspberry)
- **Strawberry**: Renovate beds after harvest — mow tops, narrow rows, remove runners.
- **Raspberry**: Remove spent floricanes (summer-bearing) or cut all canes to ground (primocane-bearing) in late winter.
- **Remove**: Any canes showing signs of cane blight, spur blight, or anthracnose.

## Tool Sanitation
- Disinfect pruning tools between plants (or between cuts on diseased wood).
- Recommended disinfectants: 10% bleach solution, 70% isopropyl alcohol, Lysol, or quaternary ammonium compounds.
- Dip or spray-wipe for 30 seconds between cuts.
- Rinse and oil tools after sanitizing with bleach to prevent corrosion.
