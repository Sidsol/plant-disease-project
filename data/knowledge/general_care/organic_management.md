# Organic Disease Management

## Principles of Organic Disease Management
Organic disease management focuses on prevention, soil health, and biological approaches rather than synthetic chemical interventions. The goal is to create a balanced ecosystem where plant vigor and natural defenses minimize disease impact.

## Approved Organic Treatments

### Copper-Based Fungicides
- **Bordeaux mixture** (copper sulfate + hydrated lime): broad-spectrum, protectant fungicide. Effective against many bacterial and fungal diseases.
- **Fixed copper** (copper hydroxide, copper octanoate): less phytotoxic than Bordeaux. Apply preventatively.
- **Caution**: Copper accumulates in soil with repeated use; excessive copper is toxic to soil organisms and earthworms. Use the minimum effective rate.

### Sulfur
- Effective against powdery mildew, scab, and some rusts.
- Apply as wettable sulfur, lime-sulfur, or dust.
- **Do not apply** when temperatures exceed 90°F (32°C) — phytotoxicity risk.
- **Do not apply** within 2 weeks of oil-based sprays (sulfur + oil = phytotoxicity).

### Neem Oil
- Derived from the neem tree (*Azadirachta indica*).
- Fungicidal, insecticidal, and miticide properties.
- Effective against powdery mildew, rust, and some leaf spots.
- Apply in the evening to avoid photodegradation and leaf burn.

### Potassium Bicarbonate
- Products: MilStop, Kaligreen, Armicarb.
- Disrupts fungal cell walls on contact.
- Effective against powdery mildew and some other foliar diseases.
- Low toxicity, rapid breakdown.

### Biological Controls
- ***Bacillus subtilis*** (Serenade): suppresses many fungal and bacterial diseases.
- ***Bacillus amyloliquefaciens*** (Double Nickel): similar spectrum to *B. subtilis*.
- ***Trichoderma*** spp.: soil-applied biocontrol agents that colonize roots and outcompete pathogens. Also applied to pruning wounds to prevent trunk diseases.
- **Compost tea**: properly prepared aerobic compost tea may suppress foliar diseases through diverse microbial competition.

### Other Organic Options
- **Milk sprays** (10-40% whole or skim milk solution): University research from Brazil, Australia, and the U.S. has consistently shown that weekly milk sprays reduce powdery mildew severity by 50-90% on cucurbits and grapes. The mechanism involves milk proteins that have antifungal properties when exposed to UV light, and beneficial microorganisms in milk that compete with mildew. Use fresh whole or skim milk diluted 1:9 to 4:6 with water. Spray both leaf surfaces weekly. This is particularly practical for home gardeners — inexpensive, non-toxic, and surprisingly effective.
- **Hydrogen peroxide** (dilute solutions, 1-3%): Oxidizing agent with broad antimicrobial activity but very short residual. Must be reapplied frequently. Not a substitute for other management practices.
- **Essential oils** (thyme, oregano, clove): Some research-backed fungicidal activity, but efficacy is variable and short-lived in field conditions. More effective in greenhouse settings.
- **Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate)**: Often recommended in home gardening websites but **less effective than potassium bicarbonate** and can cause sodium buildup in soil with repeated use. Potassium bicarbonate is the preferred alternative.
- **Compost tea**: Properly prepared aerobic compost tea may suppress foliar diseases through diverse microbial competition. However, extension programs note that results are highly variable and dependent on compost quality, brewing method, and application timing. Not a reliable standalone treatment.

## Building Plant Immunity Organically
- Maintain soil health through composting and organic matter additions.
- Balanced nutrition: soil testing followed by organic amendments (bone meal, blood meal, greensand, kelp, etc.).
- Adequate calcium for strong cell walls (gypsum, limestone).
- Mycorrhizal inoculants improve nutrient uptake and can enhance disease resistance.
- Avoid stressing plants — consistent watering, proper spacing, appropriate varieties for the climate.
