# Crop Rotation for Disease Management

## Why Rotate Crops?
Crop rotation is one of the oldest and most effective disease management strategies. Many plant pathogens survive in soil and crop debris between seasons. By planting different crop families in sequence, you starve pathogens of their host, break disease cycles, and improve soil health.

## Key Principles
1. **Rotate by plant family** — pathogens tend to be family-specific.
2. **Minimum 3-year rotation** — most pathogens decline significantly after 2-3 years without a host.
3. **Longer rotations are better** — some pathogens (e.g., clubroot of brassicas) can persist for 7+ years.
4. **Manage within-family grouping** — tomatoes, peppers, potatoes, and eggplant are all Solanaceae; rotating tomato with pepper provides little disease benefit.

## Plant Families and Rotation Groups

| Family | Common Crops | Key Diseases | Minimum Rotation |
|--------|-------------|--------------|------------------|
| **Solanaceae** | Tomato, pepper, potato, eggplant | Early blight, late blight, bacterial spot, verticillium wilt | 3-4 years |
| **Cucurbitaceae** | Squash, cucumber, melon, pumpkin | Powdery mildew, downy mildew, Fusarium wilt | 2-3 years |
| **Poaceae (Grasses)** | Corn, wheat, rice | Gray leaf spot, northern leaf blight, rust | 1-2 years |
| **Rosaceae** | Apple, cherry, peach, strawberry | Scab, black rot, brown rot | Perennial — manage differently |
| **Vitaceae** | Grape | Black rot, downy mildew, esca | Perennial — manage differently |
| **Fabaceae** | Soybean, bean, pea | White mold, root rot | 2-3 years |
| **Rutaceae** | Citrus (orange, lemon) | Citrus greening, canker | Perennial — manage differently |

## Rotation Example (4-Year Vegetable Garden)
- **Year 1**: Tomatoes, peppers (Solanaceae)
- **Year 2**: Squash, cucumbers (Cucurbitaceae)
- **Year 3**: Corn, grains (Poaceae)
- **Year 4**: Beans, peas (Fabaceae) — also fixes nitrogen for next year's heavy feeders

## Special Considerations
- **Perennial crops** (fruit trees, grapes, berries) cannot be rotated in the traditional sense. Manage through sanitation, resistant cultivars, and IPM.
- **Cover crops** between rotations improve soil health and can suppress soil-borne diseases. Mustard family cover crops release biofumigant compounds.
- **Volunteer plants**: remove self-sown tomatoes, potatoes, etc. from the previous year's planting area — they break the rotation.
- **Solanaceous weeds** (nightshade, ground cherry, jimsonweed) host the same pathogens as cultivated solanaceous crops.
