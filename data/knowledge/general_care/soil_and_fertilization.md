# Soil Health and Fertilization

## Soil Fundamentals
Healthy soil is the foundation of healthy plants. Good soil provides nutrients, water, air, and physical support to roots. Plants growing in healthy soil have stronger immune responses and are more resistant to disease.

## Soil Testing
- Test soil every 2-3 years for pH, organic matter, and nutrient levels (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, micronutrients).
- Contact your local agricultural extension office for soil testing services.
- Adjust pH to the optimal range for your crops:
  - Most vegetables: pH 6.0-7.0
  - Potatoes: pH 5.0-6.0 (lower pH reduces scab)
  - Blueberries: pH 4.5-5.5
  - Most fruit trees: pH 6.0-7.0
  - Grapes: pH 5.5-6.5

## Macronutrients

### Nitrogen (N)
- Essential for leaf growth, chlorophyll production, and overall vigor.
- Deficiency signs: pale green to yellow leaves, stunted growth, starting from older leaves.
- Excess: lush, dark green growth that is soft and susceptible to diseases and pests; delayed fruiting.
- Sources: ammonium nitrate, urea, composted manure, blood meal, feather meal.

### Phosphorus (P)
- Essential for root development, flowering, fruiting, and energy transfer.
- Deficiency signs: purplish discoloration of leaves, poor root growth, delayed maturity.
- Sources: superphosphate, bone meal, rock phosphate.

### Potassium (K)
- Essential for disease resistance, water regulation, and fruit quality.
- Deficiency signs: marginal leaf scorch (brown edges), weak stems, poor fruit quality.
- Important: potassium-deficient plants are significantly more susceptible to many diseases.
- Sources: potassium chloride (muriate of potash), potassium sulfate, wood ash, greensand.

## Micronutrients
- **Calcium**: Prevents blossom end rot in tomatoes and peppers. Ensure adequate soil calcium and consistent watering.
- **Magnesium**: Component of chlorophyll. Deficiency: interveinal yellowing of older leaves. Source: Epsom salt (magnesium sulfate), dolomitic limestone.
- **Iron**: Deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis of young leaves. Common in alkaline soils. Source: iron chelates, sulfur to lower pH.
- **Boron, Zinc, Manganese, Copper, Molybdenum**: Required in trace amounts. Deficiencies are uncommon but can be corrected with foliar sprays or soil amendments.

## Organic Matter and Composting
- Maintain 3-5% organic matter in garden soils.
- Compost improves soil structure, water retention, drainage, and microbial activity.
- Well-composted material suppresses soil-borne diseases through beneficial microbial competition.
- Apply 1-2 inches of compost annually, incorporated into the top 6 inches of soil.
- Do NOT compost diseased plant material — pathogens may survive unless compost reaches 140°F (60°C) for extended periods.

## Fertilization and Disease Interaction
- **Excess nitrogen** promotes lush, soft growth that is more susceptible to fungal and bacterial infections.
- **Adequate potassium** strengthens cell walls and improves disease resistance.
- **Balanced nutrition** is key — both deficiency and excess can increase disease susceptibility.
- **Slow-release fertilizers** provide steady nutrition without growth surges.
