# Healthy Plant Maintenance Guide

## When Your Plant Is Diagnosed as Healthy
Congratulations! Your plant appears healthy based on our AI analysis. Here's how to keep it that way.

## General Maintenance for Healthy Plants

### Regular Monitoring
- Inspect leaves weekly — both upper and lower surfaces — for early signs of spots, discoloration, wilting, or pest damage.
- Check stems and fruit for lesions, cankers, or unusual growth.
- Monitor soil moisture and plant vigor.
- Early detection of problems is always the key to successful management.

### Watering
- Water consistently and appropriately for your specific crop.
- Use drip irrigation or water at the base of plants when possible.
- Water in the morning to allow foliage to dry during the day.
- Maintain consistent moisture — fluctuating wet/dry cycles stress plants.

### Nutrition
- Follow soil test recommendations for fertilization.
- Maintain balanced nutrition — avoid excess nitrogen.
- Ensure adequate potassium, which strengthens disease resistance.
- Apply compost annually to improve soil health and microbial activity.

### Air Circulation
- Maintain proper spacing between plants.
- Prune to open canopy for light and air penetration.
- Remove weeds that compete for resources and trap moisture.
- Stake or cage tall plants (tomatoes, peppers) to keep them upright.

### Sanitation
- Remove fallen leaves and debris regularly.
- Clean tools between plants, especially after working with any suspect plant material.
- Remove and properly dispose of any plant material showing disease symptoms.
- Do not compost diseased material unless your compost reaches high temperatures (>140°F / 60°C).

### Preventive Practices
- Rotate crops annually (especially vegetables).
- Choose disease-resistant varieties when planting.
- Use certified, disease-free seed and transplants.
- Mulch to prevent soil splash and maintain moisture.

## Seasonal Checkpoints
- **Spring**: Inspect for overwintered problems; begin monitoring program; apply preventive treatments if historically needed.
- **Summer**: Peak disease season — increase monitoring frequency; maintain spray programs on susceptible crops.
- **Fall**: Clean up garden thoroughly; plan rotations for next year.
- **Winter**: Plan, order disease-resistant varieties, clean and maintain tools.
