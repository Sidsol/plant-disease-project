# Seasonal Plant Care Calendar

## Spring (March-May)

### Early Spring
- **Soil preparation**: Test soil pH and nutrients. Amend as needed before planting.
- **Pruning**: Complete dormant pruning of fruit trees, grapes, and berries before bud break.
- **Sanitation**: Rake fallen leaves, remove mummified fruit, destroy overwintered disease sources.
- **Dormant sprays**: Apply copper or lime-sulfur dormant sprays on fruit trees before bud break.
- **Plan**: Order disease-resistant varieties. Review last season's problems and adjust rotation plans.

### Mid-Late Spring
- **Planting**: Transplant warm-season crops (tomatoes, peppers) after last frost.
- **First sprays**: Begin fungicide programs on susceptible crops as growth begins.
- **Scouting**: Begin weekly inspections for early disease signs.
- **Mulching**: Apply 2-4 inches of mulch around plants to prevent soil splash and conserve moisture.

## Summer (June-August)

### Disease Management
- **Monitor closely**: Warm, humid conditions favor most fungal and bacterial diseases.
- **Spray programs**: Maintain fungicide schedules during wet weather.
- **Cultural practices**: Remove infected leaves, improve air circulation, manage irrigation.
- **Pest monitoring**: Watch for insect vectors (aphids, whiteflies, psyllids) that transmit viral diseases.

### Watering
- Irrigate consistently, especially during drought stress.
- Water in the morning. Use drip irrigation whenever possible.
- Monitor for both overwatering and underwatering symptoms.

### Harvest
- Harvest fruit and vegetables promptly to reduce disease pressure.
- Remove and destroy any infected fruit rather than leaving it in the garden.

## Fall (September-November)

### Post-Harvest
- **Clean up**: Remove all crop debris from the garden or field.
- **Composting**: Compost healthy material; burn or bag diseased material.
- **Soil amendments**: Apply lime (if needed for pH adjustment), compost, or cover crop seed.
- **Fall pruning**: Remove dead or diseased wood from perennials.
- **Leaf management**: Rake and destroy fallen leaves from fruit trees to reduce overwintering inoculum.

### Fall Sprays
- Apply copper at leaf fall on stone fruits to protect against bacterial diseases.
- Apply urea to fallen apple leaves to accelerate decomposition and reduce scab inoculum.

## Winter (December-February)

### Planning
- Review the season's disease notes. Identify problem areas and plan changes.
- Order seeds and plants — prioritize disease-resistant varieties.
- Clean, sharpen, and disinfect tools.
- Plan crop rotation for the coming season.

### Dormant Season Tasks
- Prune fruit trees and grape vines during dry weather.
- Remove and destroy mummified fruit and cankered wood.
- Apply dormant oil sprays to fruit trees for overwintering pests.
- Inspect stored produce for rot and remove affected items.
