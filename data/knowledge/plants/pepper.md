# Bell Pepper Care Guide

## General Information
Bell peppers (*Capsicum annuum*) are warm-season vegetables in the Solanaceae family. They require full sun, warm temperatures, and consistent moisture. They share many diseases with tomatoes, so rotation between the two provides little disease benefit.

## Common Diseases
- **Bacterial Spot** (*Xanthomonas* spp.): Water-soaked leaf spots, scabby fruit lesions.
- **Phytophthora Blight** (*Phytophthora capsici*): Rapid wilting, crown rot, fruit rot.
- **Anthracnose** (*Colletotrichum* spp.): Sunken, dark, circular fruit lesions.
- **Powdery Mildew**: White powdery growth on leaves.
- **Blossom End Rot**: Physiological disorder from calcium/moisture imbalance.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun, 6-8+ hours daily.
- **Soil**: Well-drained, fertile, pH 6.0-6.8.
- **Temperature**: 70-85°F (21-29°C) optimal. Do not transplant until soil is warm (65°F / 18°C).
- **Spacing**: 18-24 inches apart, rows 24-36 inches apart.
- **Watering**: 1-1.5 inches per week. Consistent moisture prevents blossom end rot.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. Use certified disease-free seed and transplants.
2. Rotate with non-solanaceous crops for 2-3 years.
3. Use drip irrigation — avoid overhead watering.
4. Mulch to prevent soil splash.
5. Apply copper-based bactericides preventively during wet periods.
6. Ensure good drainage — Phytophthora thrives in waterlogged soil.
