# Corn (Maize) Care Guide

## General Information
Corn (*Zea mays*) is the most widely grown crop in the Americas and a major global staple. It is a warm-season grass requiring full sun, adequate moisture, and good fertility. Disease management is primarily achieved through hybrid selection and crop rotation.

## Common Diseases
- **Gray Leaf Spot** (*Cercospora zeae-maydis*): Rectangular, gray-tan lesions parallel to leaf veins.
- **Common Rust** (*Puccinia sorghi*): Cinnamon-brown pustules on both leaf surfaces.
- **Northern Leaf Blight** (*Exserohilum turcicum*): Long, cigar-shaped, gray-green to tan lesions.
- **Southern Rust** (*Puccinia polysora*): Small, densely packed, orange pustules mainly on upper leaf surface.
- **Stalk Rots** (various fungi): Premature plant death and stalk breakage near harvest.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun.
- **Soil**: Rich, well-drained, pH 5.8-7.0. Corn is a heavy nitrogen feeder.
- **Temperature**: Plant when soil temperature reaches 60°F (16°C). Optimal growth at 77-91°F (25-33°C).
- **Spacing**: 8-12 inches within rows; 30-36 inch row spacing.
- **Watering**: 1-1.5 inches per week; critical demand at tasseling/silking.

## Annual Care Practices
- **Hybrid selection**: Choose hybrids with resistance to diseases prevalent in your area (GLS, NCLB, rust ratings).
- **Planting**: Plant in blocks (minimum 4 rows) for wind pollination.
- **Fertilization**: Apply nitrogen at planting and side-dress at V6. Total N: 150-200 lbs/acre for field corn.
- **Scouting**: Begin at V8 for foliar diseases; weekly through grain fill.
- **Harvest**: Monitor grain moisture; harvest at 15-25% moisture for field corn.
- **Residue management**: Tillage or rotation to manage residue-borne diseases.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. Select disease-resistant hybrids — the most cost-effective strategy.
2. Rotate with non-host crops (soybeans, small grains) to reduce residue-borne inoculum.
3. Till or manage residue in fields with heavy disease pressure.
4. Apply foliar fungicides when warranted (susceptible hybrids, high disease pressure, high-value crop).
5. Maintain balanced nutrition — stressed plants are more susceptible.
