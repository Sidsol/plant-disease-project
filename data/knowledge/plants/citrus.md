# Citrus (Orange) Care Guide

## General Information
Citrus trees (*Citrus sinensis* — sweet orange, and related species) are subtropical to tropical evergreen trees. They require warm winters (frost-free), full sun, and well-drained acidic soil. Huanglongbing (citrus greening) is currently the most devastating threat to citrus worldwide.

## Common Diseases
- **Huanglongbing / Citrus Greening** (*Ca.* Liberibacter asiaticus): Blotchy leaf yellowing, misshapen bitter fruit, tree decline. No cure.
- **Citrus Canker** (*Xanthomonas citri*): Raised, corky, crater-like lesions on leaves, stems, and fruit.
- **Citrus Black Spot** (*Phyllosticta citricarpa*): Hard, sunken spots on fruit rind.
- **Phytophthora Root Rot / Gummosis**: Bark cracking and gum exudation at trunk base.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun, 8+ hours daily.
- **Soil**: Well-drained, slightly acidic (pH 5.5-6.5). Raised beds in heavy soils.
- **Temperature**: Optimal 70-90°F (21-32°C). Most citrus is damaged below 28°F (-2°C).
- **Watering**: Deep but infrequent; allow soil to dry between waterings. Avoid waterlogging.
- **Fertilization**: Citrus-specific fertilizer (high N, adequate micronutrients including Fe, Zn, Mn) 3-4 times per year.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. Use certified disease-free nursery stock from enclosed, screened facilities.
2. Control Asian citrus psyllid — the vector of citrus greening — with systemic insecticides or biological controls.
3. Remove and destroy any tree showing HLB symptoms to protect remaining trees.
4. Ensure good drainage to prevent Phytophthora root rot.
5. Report suspected citrus greening or canker to agricultural authorities immediately.
