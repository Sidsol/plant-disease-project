# Potato Care Guide

## General Information
Potatoes (*Solanum tuberosum*) are a staple crop grown worldwide. They are cool-season crops planted from seed potatoes (tuber pieces). Proper management of water, soil, and disease is critical for good tuber yield and quality.

## Common Diseases
- **Early Blight** (*Alternaria solani*): Concentric-ring ("target") lesions on older leaves.
- **Late Blight** (*Phytophthora infestans*): Rapidly expanding water-soaked lesions; extremely destructive.
- **Common Scab** (*Streptomyces scabiei*): Rough, corky lesions on tuber skin.
- **Black Leg** (*Pectobacterium* spp.): Blackened stems at soil line; soft rot of seed tubers.
- **Verticillium Wilt** (*Verticillium dahliae*): Yellowing and wilting from the base up.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun.
- **Soil**: Well-drained, loose, sandy loam. pH 5.0-6.0 (lower pH reduces scab).
- **Temperature**: Cool season crop; optimal 60-70°F (16-21°C). Plant 2-4 weeks before last frost.
- **Spacing**: 12 inches apart in rows 30-36 inches apart.
- **Watering**: 1-2 inches per week. Consistent moisture during tuber formation (bloom stage) is critical.

## Annual Care Practices
- **Seed selection**: Use certified disease-free seed potatoes. Cut seed pieces should have 2-3 eyes and weigh 1.5-2 oz.
- **Hilling**: Mound soil around stems as plants grow (3-4 times during season) to protect tubers from light and spore exposure.
- **Fertilization**: Apply balanced fertilizer at planting; side-dress with nitrogen at hilling.
- **Scouting**: Monitor for early blight, late blight, and Colorado potato beetle.
- **Vine kill**: Mow or desiccate vines 2-3 weeks before harvest to allow skin to set.
- **Harvest**: Dig tubers carefully when soil is dry. Cure at 45-60°F for 1-2 weeks.
- **Cleanup**: Remove all volunteer tubers and plant debris. Destroy cull piles.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. **Use certified seed potatoes only** — never plant grocery store potatoes, which may carry latent infections and have been treated with sprout inhibitors.
2. **Rotate with non-solanaceous crops for 3+ years** — potatoes, tomatoes, peppers, and eggplant share many of the same pathogens.
3. **Hill adequately** to protect developing tubers — exposed tubers can be infected by late blight spores washing down from foliage during rain.
4. **Monitor weather** for late blight conditions (cool, wet, overcast); apply fungicides preventively if forecasting systems indicate risk. Check USABlight.org for regional outbreak tracking.
5. **Destroy volunteer potatoes and cull piles** — these are the primary source of late blight inoculum each spring. Even one overlooked volunteer potato can produce enough spores to infect an entire community.

**Practical growing tips**:
- Plant seed pieces 4 inches deep, 12 inches apart. Cut seed pieces should weigh about 2 oz with at least 2 eyes each. Let cut surfaces callus for 1-2 days before planting to prevent rot.
- **Lower soil pH to 5.0-5.5** to suppress common scab (*Streptomyces scabiei*). Do NOT lime potato ground — alkaline soil dramatically increases scab severity.
- Begin hilling when plants are 6 inches tall and repeat 2-3 more times during the season.
- **Vine kill timing**: Mow or desiccate vines 2-3 weeks before harvest to let skin set ("cure"). This prevents skinning during harvest and reduces storage diseases.
- Harvest in dry conditions. Wet harvest leads to storage rots.
