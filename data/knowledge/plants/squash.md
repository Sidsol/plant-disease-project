# Squash and Cucurbit Care Guide

## General Information
Squash (*Cucurbita* spp.) and other cucurbits (cucumber, melon, pumpkin) are warm-season crops requiring full sun, warm soil, and ample space. Powdery mildew is the most common disease across all cucurbit crops.

## Common Diseases
- **Powdery Mildew** (*Podosphaera xanthii*): White powdery growth on leaves.
- **Downy Mildew** (*Pseudoperonospora cubensis*): Angular yellow leaf spots with purplish fuzz beneath.
- **Bacterial Wilt** (via cucumber beetle vector): Rapid wilting of vines.
- **Phytophthora Fruit Rot**: Soft rot on fruit touching wet soil.
- **Mosaic Viruses**: Mottled, distorted leaves; aphid-transmitted.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun.
- **Soil**: Fertile, well-drained, pH 6.0-7.0. Amend heavily with compost.
- **Temperature**: Plant after last frost when soil is at least 65°F (18°C).
- **Spacing**: Winter squash 4-6 feet apart; summer squash 3-4 feet.
- **Watering**: 1-1.5 inches per week at the base of plants.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. Plant powdery mildew-resistant varieties — widely available for most cucurbit types.
2. Ensure good air circulation through proper spacing.
3. Water at the base; avoid wetting foliage.
4. Control cucumber beetles early to prevent bacterial wilt.
5. Mulch to prevent soil-borne diseases from splashing onto fruit.
6. Rotate cucurbits out of the bed for 2-3 years.
