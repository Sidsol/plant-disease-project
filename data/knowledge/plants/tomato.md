# Tomato Care Guide

## General Information
Tomatoes (*Solanum lycopersicum*) are the most popular home garden vegetable/fruit. They are warm-season crops requiring full sun, warm temperatures, and consistent moisture. Tomatoes are susceptible to numerous diseases, making proper care and prevention essential.

## Common Diseases
- **Early Blight** (*Alternaria solani*): Target-shaped lesions on lower leaves.
- **Late Blight** (*Phytophthora infestans*): Large water-soaked lesions; can destroy plants in days.
- **Septoria Leaf Spot** (*Septoria lycopersici*): Numerous small spots with tan centers and dark borders.
- **Bacterial Spot** (*Xanthomonas* spp.): Small dark spots on leaves, scabby fruit lesions.
- **Leaf Mold** (*Passalora fulva*): Yellow upper-leaf spots with olive fungal growth underneath (greenhouse).
- **Target Spot** (*Corynespora cassiicola*): Brown concentric-ring lesions.
- **Spider Mites** (*Tetranychus urticae*): Stippling, bronzing, fine webbing on undersides.
- **Tomato Mosaic Virus** (ToMV): Mottled leaves, distortion, no cure.
- **Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus** (TYLCV): Upward leaf curl, stunting, whitefly-transmitted.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun, 6-8+ hours daily.
- **Soil**: Rich, well-drained, pH 6.0-6.8. Amend with compost.
- **Temperature**: Daytime 70-85°F (21-29°C); nighttime above 55°F (13°C). Fruit set drops above 90°F.
- **Spacing**: 24-36 inches apart in rows 4-5 feet apart.
- **Watering**: 1-2 inches per week. Consistent moisture prevents blossom end rot and cracking.

## Annual Care Practices
- **Transplant**: After last frost, when soil is at least 60°F (16°C).
- **Staking/Caging**: Support plants to keep fruit and foliage off the ground.
- **Pruning**: Remove suckers below first flower cluster (indeterminate types). Remove lower leaves touching soil.
- **Mulch**: 2-4 inches of straw or plastic mulch to prevent soil splash.
- **Fertilize**: Side-dress with balanced fertilizer at first fruit set.
- **Harvest**: Pick fruit when fully colored. Remove any diseased fruit immediately.
- **End of season**: Remove and destroy all plant debris. Do not compost diseased material.

## Disease-Resistant Varieties
- **Early/Late Blight**: Mountain Merit, Mountain Magic, Defiant PhR, Iron Lady, Plum Regal.
- **Tomato Mosaic Virus**: Most modern varieties carry Tm-2² resistance.
- **TYLCV**: Varieties with Ty-1, Ty-2, Ty-3 resistance genes.
- **Bacterial Spot**: Limited resistance; management focuses on cultural + copper sprays.
- **Fusarium/Verticillium**: Look for "VFN" or "VFNT" on seed labels.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. **Rotate**: Don't plant tomatoes (or any solanaceous crop — peppers, potatoes, eggplant) in the same spot for 3+ years. This is the most frequently violated rule in home gardens.
2. **Water at the base** — never overhead. Drip irrigation or soaker hoses reduce foliar disease by 60-80% compared to sprinklers.
3. **Mulch to prevent soil splash** — 2-4 inches of straw, wood chips, or plastic mulch creates a barrier between soil-borne spores and lower leaves.
4. **Remove lower leaves** and maintain air flow. Prune everything below the lowest fruit cluster.
5. **Sanitize tools** between plants, especially when handling multiple plants. Use 70% isopropyl alcohol (less corrosive than bleach) or 20% nonfat dry milk solution.
6. **Use resistant varieties** whenever possible — check seed catalogs for VFN (Verticillium, Fusarium, Nematode), VFNT (adds Tobacco Mosaic), and Ty (TYLCV resistance) designations.

**Common mistakes that promote disease**:
- Planting tomatoes in the same bed year after year ("but I amended the soil!" — doesn't matter, pathogens persist)
- Using overhead sprinklers, especially in the evening
- Crowding plants too close together for "more yield" (actually reduces yield AND increases disease)
- Ignoring lower-leaf symptoms until the disease has reached the fruit
- Composting obviously diseased plant material (most home compost piles don't reach the 140°F needed to kill pathogens)
