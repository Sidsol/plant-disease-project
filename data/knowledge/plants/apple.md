# Apple Tree Care Guide

## General Information
Apples (*Malus domestica*) are one of the most widely grown fruit trees in temperate regions. They require full sun, well-drained soil (pH 6.0-7.0), and cross-pollination from a compatible variety. Proper care and disease management are essential for consistent fruit production.

## Common Diseases
- **Apple Scab** (*Venturia inaequalis*): Olive-green to brown velvety leaf spots and scabby fruit lesions.
- **Black Rot** (*Botryosphaeria obtusa*): Frog-eye leaf spot, fruit rot with concentric rings, and bark cankers.
- **Cedar Apple Rust** (*Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae*): Orange-yellow leaf spots; requires juniper alternate host.
- **Fire Blight** (*Erwinia amylovora*): Shoots wilt and turn black, forming a "shepherd's crook."
- **Powdery Mildew** (*Podosphaera leucotricha*): White powdery coating on new shoots and leaves.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun (6-8+ hours daily).
- **Soil**: Well-drained loam, pH 6.0-7.0. Avoid heavy clay or waterlogged areas.
- **Spacing**: Standard trees 25-30 feet apart; semi-dwarf 12-15 feet; dwarf 6-10 feet.
- **Pollination**: Most varieties need a compatible pollinator within 100 feet.
- **Watering**: 1-2 inches per week during growing season. Deep, infrequent watering preferred.

## Annual Care Calendar
- **Late winter**: Dormant pruning (open center or central leader); apply dormant oil spray.
- **Green tip**: First fungicide application for scab (captan or myclobutanil).
- **Pink to bloom**: Continue spray program; watch for fire blight if warm and wet.
- **Post-bloom**: Thin fruit to 1 per cluster for better size and to reduce disease harbor.
- **Summer**: Maintain spray program, irrigate during drought, monitor for pests.
- **Harvest**: Pick fruit at maturity; remove mummified fruit.
- **Fall**: Rake and destroy fallen leaves (scab sanitation); apply urea to leaf litter.

## Disease-Resistant Varieties
Liberty, Enterprise, Freedom, Redfree, Pristine, Williams Pride, GoldRush, Crimson Crisp.
