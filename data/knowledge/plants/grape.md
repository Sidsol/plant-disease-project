# Grape Care Guide

## General Information
Grapes (*Vitis vinifera*, *V. labrusca*, and hybrids) are perennial fruiting vines grown for fresh eating, wine, juice, and raisins. They require full sun, good air circulation, well-drained soil, and a structured training/trellis system. Disease management is a central concern in grape production.

## Common Diseases
- **Black Rot** (*Guignardia bidwellii*): Tan leaf spots and shriveled, mummified fruit.
- **Esca / Black Measles**: Tiger-stripe leaf symptoms, vine decline, no curative treatment.
- **Leaf Blight / Isariopsis Leaf Spot** (*Pseudocercospora vitis*): Dark brown leaf spots with fungal sporulation.
- **Downy Mildew** (*Plasmopara viticola*): Yellow "oil spots" on upper leaf, white fuzz below.
- **Powdery Mildew** (*Erysiphe necator*): White powdery growth on leaves, shoots, and berries.
- **Botrytis Bunch Rot** (*Botrytis cinerea*): Gray fuzzy mold on ripening fruit clusters.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun, at least 6-8 hours daily. Good air circulation is critical.
- **Soil**: Well-drained; pH 5.5-6.5. Rocky or gravelly soil is often preferred.
- **Training**: Requires a trellis or support system. Common systems: VSP, Geneva Double Curtain, high cordon.
- **Spacing**: 6-10 feet between vines; 8-12 feet between rows.
- **Watering**: Established vines are somewhat drought-tolerant. Water deeply during drought; avoid overhead irrigation.

## Annual Care Calendar
- **Late winter/Early spring**: Prune dormant vines; remove mummified fruit and cankered canes. Apply dormant sprays if needed.
- **Bud break to bloom**: Begin fungicide program for black rot, downy mildew, and powdery mildew.
- **Post-bloom to veraison**: Critical spray window for fruit diseases. Canopy management (shoot positioning, leaf removal).
- **Veraison to harvest**: Monitor for botrytis; manage canopy for air flow.
- **Post-harvest**: Clean up fallen fruit and leaves. Fall fertilization if needed.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. Sanitation is paramount — remove mummified fruit, cankered wood, and fallen debris.
2. Open canopy management reduces humidity and disease pressure.
3. Protect pruning wounds from esca pathogens.
4. Follow a consistent fungicide schedule during the growing season.
5. Choose disease-resistant varieties when possible (e.g., French-American hybrids for humid climates).
