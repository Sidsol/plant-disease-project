# Cherry Tree Care Guide

## General Information
Cherry trees (*Prunus avium* — sweet cherry; *Prunus cerasus* — sour/tart cherry) are popular stone fruits requiring cold winters and well-drained soil. They are susceptible to several fungal and bacterial diseases.

## Common Diseases
- **Powdery Mildew** (*Podosphaera clandestina*): White powdery growth on leaves and shoots.
- **Brown Rot** (*Monilinia* spp.): Fruit rot with tan/brown fuzzy sporulation.
- **Cherry Leaf Spot** (*Blumeriella jaapii*): Small purple-red spots leading to yellowing and defoliation.
- **Bacterial Canker** (*Pseudomonas syringae*): Dark, sunken bark cankers; gummosis.

## Growing Requirements
- **Sun**: Full sun.
- **Soil**: Deep, well-drained, pH 6.0-7.0. Avoid heavy clay.
- **Chill hours**: Sweet cherry: 700-900 hours below 45°F. Sour cherry: 600-700 hours.
- **Pollination**: Sweet cherries generally need a pollinator; sour cherries are mostly self-fertile.
- **Spacing**: Standard 20-30 feet; dwarf 8-14 feet.

## Key Disease Prevention Tips
1. Ensure good air circulation through open-center pruning.
2. Apply sulfur or myclobutanil for powdery mildew starting at petal fall.
3. Remove and destroy mummified fruit (brown rot).
4. Avoid wounding bark — bacterial canker enters through wounds.
5. Prune during dry weather to prevent disease spread.
