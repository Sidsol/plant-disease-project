# Strawberry Leaf Scorch (Diplocarpon earlianum)

## Overview
Leaf scorch of strawberry is caused by the fungus *Diplocarpon earlianum*. It is one of several common foliar diseases of strawberry (alongside leaf spot and leaf blight) and can cause substantial weakening of plants through premature defoliation. The disease is most damaging in warm, humid regions and in perennial matted-row production systems.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Small (1-5 mm), irregular, dark purple spots develop on the upper leaf surface. Unlike leaf spot (*Mycosphaerella fragariae*), leaf scorch spots do not develop a white center — the entire lesion remains dark purple to reddish-brown. Multiple spots coalesce, giving the leaf a "scorched" appearance.
- **Calyxes and Petioles**: Dark purple lesions may also develop on fruit caps and petioles.
- **Severe infections**: Heavy infection leads to complete defoliation. Repeated cycles of defoliation weaken crowns, reduce winter hardiness, and lower yields the following season.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in infected, dead, and living leaves.
2. In spring, acervuli on overwintered leaves produce conidia that are splash-dispersed to new growth.
3. Infection requires leaf wetness and warm temperatures (77-85°F / 25-30°C).
4. New lesions produce conidia within 1-2 weeks, enabling secondary spread throughout the season.
5. Disease is most severe during warm, wet summers.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply captan or thiram at bloom and again post-harvest.
- Myclobutanil (Rally) or azoxystrobin (Abound) can be used for systemic control.
- Follow a fungicide rotation program to prevent resistance.

### Cultural Practices
- Renovate beds after harvest to remove infected foliage (mow, narrow rows, remove clippings).
- Avoid overhead irrigation in the evening — irrigate in the morning to allow foliage drying.
- Ensure adequate row spacing and plant spacing for air circulation.
- Remove weeds that compete for air flow and trap moisture.
- Plant resistant or tolerant cultivars when available.

### Organic Control
- Copper-based sprays provide moderate protection.
- Maintain plant vigor through organic fertilization and proper bed management.

## Prevention
- Renovate matted-row strawberry beds annually after harvest — this is the single most important cultural practice for strawberry disease management. Mow tops to 1 inch, narrow rows to 8-12 inches, and remove clippings. This removes the vast majority of infected leaf tissue.
- Select cultivars with resistance or tolerance to leaf scorch. Consult your local extension service for varieties adapted to your region.
- Maintain good air circulation through proper spacing (12-18 inches in-row, 3-4 foot row spacing) and thorough weed management.
- Avoid working in fields when foliage is wet to prevent spreading spores on hands, clothes, and tools.

**Real-world tip**: Many home gardeners let strawberry beds become overcrowded over 2-3 years without renovation. This creates dense, humid conditions that are perfect for leaf scorch, leaf spot, and gray mold. Annual renovation after harvest is the "secret" of productive, disease-free strawberry patches. If your beds haven't been renovated in 3+ years, it's often better to start fresh with new disease-free transplants in a new location.
