# Cherry Powdery Mildew (Podosphaera clandestina)

## Overview
Powdery mildew of cherry is caused by the fungus *Podosphaera clandestina*. It affects both sweet and sour cherry trees, primarily attacking leaves and young shoots. Unlike many fungal diseases, powdery mildew thrives in warm, dry conditions with cool nights that produce high humidity.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: White to grayish powdery fungal growth appears on both upper and lower leaf surfaces. Infected leaves may curl upward, become distorted, and develop chlorotic (yellow) or necrotic (brown) patches.
- **Shoots**: Young terminal growth may be stunted and covered with white mycelium.
- **Fruit**: Rarely directly infected, but severe leaf loss reduces fruit quality and sugar content. Occasionally, a netlike russeting can appear on fruit.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters as chasmothecia (sexual fruiting bodies) on fallen leaves and bark.
2. In spring, ascospores are released and infect newly emerging leaves.
3. Secondary conidia are produced on infected tissues throughout the season, spreading via wind.
4. Optimal conditions: 70-80°F (21-27°C) with moderate humidity. Rain actually inhibits spore germination.
5. Late-season infections are generally less damaging but can weaken trees going into dormancy.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply sulfur-based sprays early in the season as a preventative measure beginning at petal fall.
- Myclobutanil or triflumizole are highly effective systemic fungicides.
- Alternate modes of action to prevent resistance development.

### Organic Control
- Sulfur sprays are approved for organic production and are the most effective organic option.
- Neem oil can be used as an organic alternative; apply at first sign of white patches.
- Potassium bicarbonate (Kaligreen, MilStop) disrupts fungal cell walls.
- Biological controls: *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade) provides moderate suppression.

### Cultural Practices
- Ensure good air circulation through proper pruning — thin dense canopy areas.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization, which promotes succulent growth susceptible to mildew.
- Remove and destroy severely infected shoots during dormant pruning.
- Irrigate in the morning so foliage dries quickly, though note that surface moisture is less critical for powdery mildew than for other fungal diseases.

## Prevention
- Select powdery mildew-resistant or tolerant cherry cultivars when planting.
- Maintain good tree vigor through balanced fertilization and irrigation.
- Monitor trees regularly from petal fall onward; early detection enables more effective treatment.

**Real-world context**: Unlike most fungal diseases, powdery mildew does NOT require free water on leaf surfaces for infection — in fact, rain and overhead irrigation can actually suppress powdery mildew by physically washing spores off leaves. The disease thrives in warm (70-80°F), moderately humid conditions with cool nights that produce heavy dew. This counterintuitive behavior means powdery mildew is often worse during warm, DRY periods — the opposite of most other fungal diseases. Home gardeners who water their cherry trees overhead during a mildew outbreak are actually helping, not hurting (though overhead watering promotes other diseases, so it's not recommended as a management strategy).
