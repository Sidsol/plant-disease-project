# Corn Cercospora / Gray Leaf Spot (Cercospora zeae-maydis)

## Overview
Gray leaf spot (GLS) is one of the most significant foliar diseases of corn (maize) worldwide. Caused by the fungus *Cercospora zeae-maydis*, it can cause yield losses exceeding 30% in susceptible hybrids under favorable conditions. The disease is most damaging in regions with warm, humid weather and reduced tillage practices.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Lesions begin as small, dark, water-soaked spots. As they mature, they elongate into rectangular, grayish-tan lesions running parallel to leaf veins. Lesions are typically 2-8 cm long and 2-4 mm wide with sharply defined, parallel edges.
- **Severe infections**: Multiple lesions may coalesce, causing large areas of leaf tissue to become necrotic. Lower leaves are typically affected first, progressing upward.
- **Timing**: Symptoms usually appear around tasseling (VT stage) and become most evident during grain fill.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in infected corn residue on the soil surface.
2. In spring and early summer, conidia (spores) are produced on residue during warm, humid conditions.
3. Spores are wind-dispersed and splash-dispersed to lower leaves.
4. Infection requires extended periods (12+ hours) of high humidity (>90%) and moderate temperatures (72-82°F / 22-28°C).
5. Secondary cycles produce more conidia that spread the disease upward through the canopy.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply foliar fungicides (strobilurins such as azoxystrobin or pyraclostrobin) at VT-R1 stage (tasseling to silking).
- Triazole fungicides (propiconazole, prothioconazole) are also effective.
- Tank mixes of strobilurin + triazole provide both protectant and curative activity.
- Fungicide application is most economical when disease pressure is moderate to high and susceptible hybrids are planted.

### Cultural Practices
- Rotate crops and avoid continuous corn planting — the pathogen survives on corn residue.
- Use resistant hybrids when available — this is the most cost-effective management strategy.
- Tillage to bury infected crop residue reduces surface inoculum.
- Maintain balanced fertility; stressed plants are more susceptible.
- Scout fields weekly during warm, humid conditions starting at V8 stage.

## Prevention
- Hybrid selection is critical: choose hybrids with good GLS resistance ratings — this is the most cost-effective management strategy and the #1 recommendation from every major corn-growing state's extension service.
- In high-risk areas (continuous corn, reduced tillage, humid climate), plan for fungicide application at VT-R1 as an insurance measure.
- Scout lower leaves beginning at V8 to detect early lesions. Rectangular lesions with straight, parallel edges are diagnostic for GLS — if lesions have wavy or irregular edges, it may be a different disease.
- **Economic threshold**: Fungicide application is most economical when: (1) a susceptible hybrid is planted, (2) previous crop was corn, (3) reduced tillage is practiced, AND (4) weather is warm and humid. If all four factors are present, the probability of profitable return on fungicide investment exceeds 80%.

**Real-world tip**: Many commercial corn growers in the U.S. Corn Belt now apply a foliar fungicide at VT as standard practice regardless of disease pressure, treating it as "yield insurance." University research shows this is economically justified only about 50% of the time. Growers who scout their fields and make data-driven spray decisions consistently outperform calendar-based sprayers.
