# Grape Black Rot (Guignardia bidwellii)

## Overview
Black rot is the most destructive disease of grapes in warm, humid regions. Caused by the fungus *Guignardia bidwellii*, it can destroy up to 80% of a grape crop in years with extended wet weather. All green parts of the vine are susceptible, but fruit losses are the primary concern.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Small, circular, tan-brown lesions (1-5 mm) with dark borders appear 10-21 days after infection. Tiny black pycnidia (fungal fruiting bodies) may be visible within the lesion.
- **Fruit**: Initially, light brown, soft spots appear. The berry rapidly shrivels and turns into a hard, black, wrinkled mummy. Mummified berries may remain attached to the cluster or fall to the ground.
- **Shoots/Tendrils**: Elongated, dark, sunken cankers may develop on young shoots and tendrils.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in mummified berries on the ground or on the vine, and in cankered shoots.
2. In spring, rain triggers the release of both ascospores and conidia from overwintered mummies.
3. Spores are splash-dispersed to new growth; infection requires 6+ hours of wetness at 60-90°F (15-32°C).
4. Fruit is most susceptible from bloom to 3-4 weeks post-bloom, becoming increasingly resistant as berries ripen.
5. Secondary conidia continue the spread throughout the growing season.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply mancozeb or myclobutanil from shoot growth through veraison on a 7-14 day schedule.
- Captan is effective as a protectant; strobilurin fungicides provide excellent control.
- The critical spray window is from immediate pre-bloom through 4 weeks post-bloom.
- Continue applications during extended wet periods.

### Cultural Practices
- Remove mummified berries from vines and ground during dormant-season pruning — this is the single most important management step. Each mummified berry can produce thousands of spores the following spring.
- Remove infected tendrils, shoots, and cane cankers.
- Ensure canopy management for good airflow — proper training (VSP, Geneva Double Curtain), shoot positioning, and leaf removal around fruit clusters all reduce humidity in the fruiting zone.
- Cultivate or mulch under vines to bury fallen mummies and reduce spore release.
- **Fruit susceptibility window**: Berries are most susceptible from bloom through 3-4 weeks post-bloom, then become increasingly resistant as they ripen. This means the spray window from immediate pre-bloom to 4 weeks post-bloom is absolutely critical — missing this window is the most common management failure.

**Real-world tip**: In the eastern U.S. and Midwest (humid climates), black rot is often the #1 grape disease problem. Growers who skip early-season sanitation and miss the bloom-window sprays often lose 50-80% of their crop. For backyard grape growers, simply removing all mummies and spraying a copper-based fungicide from bloom through 4 weeks post-bloom can dramatically reduce losses.

### Organic Control
- Copper-based fungicides (Bordeaux mixture) applied at early shoot growth and pre-bloom.
- Sulfur sprays provide moderate protection but are less effective than synthetic fungicides.

## Prevention
- Sanitation is the foundation: remove all mummified berries and cankered wood annually.
- Choose cultivars with some tolerance (e.g., some French-American hybrids are less susceptible).
- Maintain an open canopy for rapid drying of fruit and foliage.
