# Potato Early Blight (Alternaria solani)

## Overview
Early blight of potato is caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*. It is one of the most common foliar diseases of potato worldwide, occurring wherever potatoes are grown. Despite its name, it typically develops during mid to late season. The disease causes leaf spotting, defoliation, and can also infect tubers, reducing both yield and quality.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Dark brown to black circular lesions with distinctive concentric rings ("target spot" or "bull's-eye" appearance). Lesions typically appear first on older, lower leaves. A yellow halo may surround the lesion.
- **Stems**: Dark, elongated lesions develop on stems and petioles at or near the soil line.
- **Tubers**: Sunken, dark, circular lesions with raised borders and leathery brown flesh underneath. Tuber infection reduces storage quality.
- **Progressive defoliation**: Lower leaves are affected first; damage progresses upward. Severe defoliation reduces tuber size and yield.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in infected plant debris, soil, and infected tubers.
2. Conidia (spores) are produced on overwintered debris and splash- or wind-dispersed to lower leaves.
3. Infection requires alternating wet and dry periods; 1-2 hours of leaf wetness with moderate temperatures (75-85°F / 24-29°C).
4. After infection, the incubation period is 3-5 days.
5. Older, senescing, or nutrient-stressed tissue is more susceptible than young, vigorous tissue.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply chlorothalonil or mancozeb at first sign of symptoms.
- Azoxystrobin (strobilurin), boscalid, or difenoconazole provide good curative and protectant activity.
- Start applications when lower leaves show initial lesions; repeat every 7-14 days.
- Alternate fungicide classes (FRAC groups) to prevent resistance.

### Cultural Practices
- Rotate with non-solanaceous crops (the pathogen also infects tomatoes, peppers, and eggplant).
- Maintain adequate fertility, especially nitrogen — deficient plants are much more susceptible.
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris after harvest.
- Use certified, disease-free seed potatoes.
- Irrigate in the morning to allow foliage to dry during the day.
- Provide adequate spacing for air circulation.

### Organic Control
- Copper-based fungicides provide moderate protection.
- *Bacillus subtilis*-based products (Serenade) can reduce disease severity.
- Maintain plant vigor through compost and balanced organic fertilization.

## Prevention
- Plant early blight-resistant or tolerant varieties when available.
- Adequate nutrition (particularly N, K) is critical — deficiency dramatically increases susceptibility.
- Avoid planting potatoes in fields where solanaceous crops were grown the previous year.
- Hill potatoes adequately to protect tubers from spore exposure.
