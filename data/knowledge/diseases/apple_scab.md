# Apple Scab (Venturia inaequalis)

## Overview
Apple scab is one of the most common and economically important diseases of apple trees worldwide. It is caused by the fungal pathogen *Venturia inaequalis*. The disease affects leaves, fruit, and occasionally young twigs, leading to premature defoliation and unmarketable fruit.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Olive-green to dark brown velvety spots appear on the upper surface. Severely infected leaves may curl, become distorted, and drop prematurely.
- **Fruit**: Dark, scabby lesions develop on the surface. Early infections cause misshapen fruit; late-season infections produce small, pinpoint black spots (pin-point scab).
- **Twigs**: Occasionally, blistered or raised areas appear on young shoots.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in fallen infected leaves on the ground.
2. In spring, sexual spores (ascospores) are released during rain events and wind-carried to new growth.
3. Spores germinate on wet leaf surfaces and infect the tissue; moisture must persist for 9-17 hours depending on temperature (Mills period).
4. Secondary spores (conidia) are produced on primary lesions and cause new infections throughout the growing season, with new spots appearing within 9-17 days of infection.
5. The infection cycle can repeat many times throughout the growing season whenever leaves remain wet long enough.
6. By autumn, infected fallen leaves become the source of inoculum for the following year.
7. **Important**: Many ornamental crabapple trees are susceptible to apple scab and can serve as infection sources for nearby fruit trees — even trees in neighboring yards.

**Long-term impact**: Several consecutive years of early leaf loss leads to decreased growth, reduced bloom, and increased susceptibility to winter injury. Trees weakened by chronic scab are more vulnerable to secondary pests and environmental stress.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply fungicide (captan or myclobutanil) at green-tip stage, continuing through petal fall.
- Mancozeb is effective as a protectant spray applied before infection periods.
- Systemic fungicides such as myclobutanil (Eagle) or difenoconazole can be applied curatively within 72 hours of infection.
- Follow a spray schedule aligned with predicted rain events (use Mills table or apple scab prediction models).
- **For home fruit trees**: Use fungicides only on trees where fruit quality is important (apples for eating fresh) AND the tree has a history of severe apple scab. Do not apply fungicides to resistant varieties or ornamental crabapples.
- **For ornamental crabapples**: Fungicides are generally unnecessary — select a resistant variety instead. If a tree loses most leaves to scab for 3+ consecutive years, fungicide treatment may be warranted to restore health.
- Always combine fungicides with good sanitation and cultural practices — fungicides alone are not sufficient.

### Organic Control
- Sulfur-based fungicides are approved for organic production and effective when applied preventatively.
- Copper sprays (Bordeaux mixture) at green-tip can reduce early inoculum.
- Potassium bicarbonate sprays can suppress scab when applied preventatively.

### Cultural Practices
- Rake and destroy fallen leaves in autumn to reduce overwintering inoculum by up to 80%.
- Infected leaves can be burned, buried, or composted. Alternatively, chop leaves with a mulching lawn mower — a fall lawn fertilizer application will help break down chopped leaf litter faster.
- Apply urea (5% solution) to fallen leaves in autumn to accelerate decomposition of infected leaf litter.
- Plant scab-resistant cultivars such as Liberty, Enterprise, Freedom, Redfree, Pristine, or Williams Pride — this is the single most effective management strategy according to university extension services.
- **Do not overcrowd plants.** Use the mature size of the tree as a spacing guide for new plantings.
- Prune trees to create an open canopy that allows air to move through branches and dry leaves quickly. Remove upright suckers and water sprouts that have formed along the main trunk or within the canopy.
- Maintain balanced fertilization to support tree vigor without promoting excessive soft growth.
- **Note**: Even with excellent fall leaf cleanup, spores from nearby unmanaged apple or crabapple trees can travel to your property and start new infections.

## Prevention
- Select resistant cultivars when establishing new orchards.
- Maintain a sanitation program by removing fallen leaves each autumn.
- Monitor weather conditions and apply protectant fungicides ahead of rain events during the primary scab season (green tip through second cover).
- Use scab prediction models (e.g., RIMpro, Mills table) to time sprays efficiently.
