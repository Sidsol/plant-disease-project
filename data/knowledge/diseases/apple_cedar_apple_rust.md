# Apple Cedar Apple Rust (Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae)

## Overview
Cedar apple rust is caused by the fungal pathogen *Gymnosporangium juniperi-virginianae*. This disease requires two host plants to complete its life cycle: apple (or crabapple) and eastern red cedar (juniper). It is common in regions where these two hosts grow in proximity.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Bright yellow-orange spots appear on the upper leaf surface in late spring. As lesions mature, small black dots (spermogonia) form in the center. On the lower leaf surface, tube-like structures (aecia) develop, releasing powdery orange spores.
- **Fruit**: Infected fruit may develop similar orange lesions and become deformed.
- **Cedar/Juniper**: Round, reddish-brown galls (1-5 cm diameter) form on twigs. In wet spring weather, gelatinous orange "horns" (telial horns) protrude from the galls, releasing spores that infect apples.

## Disease Cycle
1. Galls on juniper produce telial horns during spring rains (April-May).
2. Basidiospores are wind-carried to apple trees, where they infect leaves and fruit.
3. Lesions on apple produce aeciospores in late summer that are carried back to juniper.
4. It takes 18-20 months for galls to mature on juniper before producing telial horns.
5. The full cycle requires both hosts; removing one breaks the cycle.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply myclobutanil or mancozeb at early bloom and repeat at 7-10 day intervals through petal fall.
- Fenarimol and triflumizole are also effective systemic options.
- Begin protectant sprays when orange galls on nearby junipers begin to swell.

### Cultural Practices
- Remove nearby juniper or red cedar hosts within 1-2 miles if possible — this breaks the disease cycle.
- Prune and destroy galls on junipers before telial horns appear in spring.
- Choose rust-resistant apple cultivars: Freedom, Redfree, Liberty, Enterprise, Williams Pride, or Pristine.
- Avoid planting susceptible apple varieties near windbreaks or hedgerows containing eastern red cedar.

### Organic Control
- Sulfur-based sprays offer partial protection when applied preventatively.
- Neem oil may provide minor suppression but is not a primary control measure.

## Prevention
- Plant rust-resistant cultivars for new orchard or garden plantings.
- Scout junipers in the area for developing galls and remove them.
- Time protectant sprays with the appearance of orange telial horns on nearby cedars.
