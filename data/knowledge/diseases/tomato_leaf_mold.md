# Tomato Leaf Mold (Passalora fulva)

## Overview
Tomato leaf mold is caused by the fungus *Passalora fulva* (syn. *Cladosporium fulvum*, *Fulvia fulva*). The disease is primarily a problem in greenhouse and high-tunnel tomato production, where high humidity and limited air circulation create ideal conditions. Field-grown tomatoes can also be affected in warm, humid climates.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves (upper surface)**: Pale green to yellowish spots of varying size develop.
- **Leaves (lower surface)**: Olive-green to grayish-brown, velvety fungal growth develops directly below the upper-surface spots. This is the most diagnostic feature.
- **Severe infections**: Spots coalesce, leaves curl upward, wither, and drop. Defoliation proceeds from the lower canopy upward.
- **Fruit**: Rarely affected directly, but occasionally dark, leathery, sunken spots may develop on the blossom end.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters on crop debris, seed, and greenhouse structures.
2. Conidia are produced abundantly on infected leaves and dispersed by air currents.
3. Infection requires high relative humidity (>85%) but does NOT require free water on leaves.
4. Optimal temperature for infection: 72-75°F (22-24°C).
5. Incubation period: 10-14 days. Sporulation follows, enabling rapid secondary spread.
6. Conidia can survive for months on greenhouse surfaces, stakes, and tools.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply chlorothalonil or mancozeb preventively at first sign of symptoms.
- Azoxystrobin or difenoconazole provide systemic control.
- In greenhouses, rotational programs are critical to prevent resistance.

### Cultural Practices
- Improve greenhouse ventilation and reduce humidity — this is the most important management step.
- Use fans, vents, and heating to keep humidity below 85%.
- Remove infected leaves promptly and remove them from the greenhouse.
- Stake plants and maintain an open canopy for air movement.
- Space plants generously.
- Sanitize greenhouse structures, stakes, and tools between seasons.
- Avoid overhead watering.

### Organic Control
- Potassium bicarbonate (MilStop, Kaligreen) sprays.
- Biological controls: *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade) may provide some suppression.
- Neem oil has limited efficacy against leaf mold.

## Prevention
- Plant resistant varieties — many greenhouse varieties carry Cf resistance genes (Cf-2, Cf-4, Cf-5, Cf-9).
- Monitor humidity closely in enclosed growing environments.
- Remove all crop debris at end of season and sanitize greenhouse.
