# Corn Northern Leaf Blight (Exserohilum turcicum)

## Overview
Northern corn leaf blight (NCLB) is caused by the fungus *Exserohilum turcicum* (syn. *Setosphaeria turcica*). It is one of the most important foliar diseases of corn, capable of causing significant yield losses (up to 30-50%) when infections occur before or during tasseling. The disease is favored by moderate temperatures, heavy dew, and high humidity.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Long (2-15 cm), elliptical, grayish-green or tan-colored lesions develop, typically oriented along the leaf axis. Mature lesions often have a characteristic cigar-shaped or canoe-shaped appearance.
- **Lesion development**: Initially, small gray-green spots appear and elongate over 1-2 weeks. Under humid conditions, dark gray to olive-green sporulation may be visible within lesions.
- **Severe infections**: Multiple lesions may coalesce, killing large portions of leaf tissue. Lower leaves are typically affected first.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters as mycelia and conidia in infected corn residue.
2. Conidia are produced on crop debris in spring and dispersed by wind and rain splash.
3. Infection requires moderate temperatures (64-80°F / 18-27°C) and extended leaf wetness (6-18 hours of dew).
4. Incubation period is 7-12 days; secondary cycles can repeat every 1-2 weeks under favorable conditions.
5. Reduced tillage and continuous corn increase disease pressure.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply strobilurin or triazole fungicides at early tassel (VT) stage.
- Popular products: azoxystrobin (Quadris), propiconazole (Tilt), or premixes (Quilt Xcel).
- Application timing is critical — fungicides are most effective when applied before disease reaches the upper canopy (ear leaf and above).
- Aerial or ground application; ensure good canopy penetration.

### Cultural Practices
- Rotate with non-host crops (soybeans, small grains) to reduce inoculum in the field.
- Tillage to bury infected crop residue accelerates decomposition and reduces spore production.
- Select hybrids with quantitative (partial) resistance for consistent multi-year protection.
- Avoid late planting, which may shift susceptible growth stages into periods of higher disease pressure.

## Prevention
- Use hybrids with Ht resistance genes (Ht1, Ht2, Ht3, HtN) for qualitative resistance, or hybrids with high quantitative resistance ratings.
- Scout fields regularly from V8 onward; early detection informs fungicide decisions.
- Consider fungicide application when susceptible hybrids show lesions on the 3rd leaf below the ear or higher before tasseling.
