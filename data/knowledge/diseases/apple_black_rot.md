# Apple Black Rot (Botryosphaeria obtusa)

## Overview
Black rot is a fungal disease of apple caused by *Botryosphaeria obtusa* (syn. *Diplodia seriata*). It affects fruit, leaves, and bark, and can cause significant losses in warm, humid climates. The fungus is one of the most common canker-causing organisms on apple trees.

## Symptoms
- **Fruit**: Brown, circular lesions expand concentrically, developing a characteristic "frog-eye" pattern with alternating dark and light brown rings. Infected fruit eventually mummifies on the tree.
- **Leaves**: "Frog-eye leaf spot" — small, circular lesions with purple margins and tan centers; may coalesce in severe infections.
- **Bark/Limbs**: Cankers appear as sunken, reddish-brown areas on limbs. The bark may crack and peel, revealing dark wood underneath.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in mummified fruit, dead wood, and bark cankers.
2. Spores are released during warm, wet weather in spring and summer.
3. Infections occur through wounds, insect damage, or natural openings (lenticels).
4. The disease progresses rapidly in warm temperatures (optimal 20-30°C / 68-86°F).

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply captan or thiophanate-methyl from pink stage through second cover spray.
- Fungicides targeting apple scab often provide concurrent black rot control.
- Strobilurin fungicides (e.g., pyraclostrobin) can also be effective.

### Cultural Practices
- Prune out dead or cankered wood and remove mummified fruit from the tree and ground.
- Maintain balanced fertility to avoid tree stress — stressed trees are more vulnerable.
- Remove wild or abandoned apple trees nearby that may serve as inoculum sources.
- Ensure good air circulation through proper pruning and spacing.
- Avoid wounding trees during cultivation and harvesting.

### Organic Control
- Copper-based sprays applied at dormant or green-tip stages can reduce inoculum.
- Neem oil has some fungistatic properties but is not highly effective against black rot.

## Prevention
- Maintain overall tree health through proper watering, fertilization, and pest management.
- Promptly remove and destroy any infected fruit, leaves, or wood.
- Keep orchard floors clean of fallen debris.
- Plant resistant or tolerant cultivars when possible.
