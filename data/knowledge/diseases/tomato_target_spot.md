# Tomato Target Spot (Corynespora cassiicola)

## Overview
Target spot of tomato is caused by the fungus *Corynespora cassiicola*. The disease has become increasingly important in tropical and subtropical tomato-growing regions. It can cause significant defoliation and direct fruit infection, reducing both yield and quality.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Brown, circular lesions with concentric rings (target-like). Lesions range from 1-10 mm and may have a yellowish halo. Lower leaves affected first, progressing upward.
- **Stems**: Elongated, dark brown lesions.
- **Fruit**: Small to medium, sunken, brown spots with concentric rings, typically near the stem attachment. Can render fruit unmarketable.
- **Distinction**: Can be confused with early blight (*Alternaria solani*) — target spot lesions tend to be smaller and more numerous, and the pathogen grows faster under tropical conditions.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus survives in crop debris and has a wide host range (including soybean, cotton, cucumber, and many weeds).
2. Conidia are wind- and splash-dispersed.
3. Optimal conditions: warm (77-86°F / 25-30°C) with high humidity.
4. Extended leaf wetness promotes infection and sporulation.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply chlorothalonil or azoxystrobin at disease onset.
- Boscalid, fluopyram, or difenoconazole are effective systemic options.
- Begin applications when first symptoms appear on lower leaves.
- Alternate chemical classes to prevent resistance.

### Cultural Practices
- Promote good air flow through staking, pruning lower branches, and proper spacing.
- Rotate crops — avoid planting after other host crops (soybean, cotton, cucurbits).
- Remove and destroy crop debris after harvest.
- Use drip irrigation and avoid overhead watering.
- Remove lower leaves showing symptoms.

## Prevention
- Select resistant or tolerant varieties when available.
- Scout lower canopy regularly during warm, humid weather.
- Maintain good crop rotation practices.
