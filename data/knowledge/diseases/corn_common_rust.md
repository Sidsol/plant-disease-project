# Corn Common Rust (Puccinia sorghi)

## Overview
Common rust of corn is caused by the obligate fungal pathogen *Puccinia sorghi*. It is widespread in temperate and subtropical corn-growing regions. While common rust rarely causes severe yield losses in field corn (typically 2-8%), sweet corn and seed corn are more vulnerable. The disease is favored by cool to moderate temperatures and high humidity.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Small, circular to elongate, cinnamon-brown to golden-brown pustules (uredinia) develop on both leaf surfaces. Pustules rupture the epidermis and release powdery, reddish-brown spores.
- **Distribution**: Pustules often appear scattered across the leaf blade, sometimes in clusters. Both upper and lower surfaces are equally affected (distinguishing it from southern rust, which favors upper surfaces).
- **Late season**: As the plant matures, dark brown to black telia (overwintering structures) may form, giving a darker appearance to the pustules.

## Disease Cycle
1. *Puccinia sorghi* does not typically overwinter in northern regions; urediniospores are wind-carried from southern regions each season.
2. Spores require free moisture and cool temperatures (60-77°F / 16-25°C) for germination and infection.
3. After initial infection, new pustules appear in 7-14 days, and each lesion can produce thousands of spores.
4. Multiple infection cycles occur throughout the growing season.
5. The sexual stage involves an alternate host (*Oxalis* spp.), but the asexual urediniospore stage is responsible for epidemic development in corn.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply foliar fungicide if rust appears before tasseling and conditions favor continued spread.
- Strobilurin fungicides (azoxystrobin, pyraclostrobin) or triazole fungicides (propiconazole) are effective.
- In sweet corn, fungicide applications are more commonly warranted due to the high value per acre.

### Cultural Practices
- Plant rust-tolerant hybrids — this is the primary management strategy for field corn.
- Adjust planting dates if possible to avoid peak infection periods.
- Scout fields weekly during warm, humid conditions, especially after storms that may carry spores from southern regions.
- Maintain adequate plant nutrition; stressed plants show more severe symptoms.

## Prevention
- Select hybrids with rust resistance genes (Rp genes) appropriate for the region.
- Monitor disease forecasting services and extension alerts for rust migration patterns.
- In sweet corn, plan for preventative fungicide applications during the tassel-to-silk period.
