# Grape Leaf Blight / Isariopsis Leaf Spot (Pseudocercospora vitis)

## Overview
Grape leaf blight, also known as Isariopsis leaf spot, is caused by the fungus *Pseudocercospora vitis* (syn. *Isariopsis clavispora*). While generally less destructive than black rot or downy mildew, severe infections can cause premature defoliation, weakening vines and reducing fruit quality. The disease is most common in warm, humid climates.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Irregular, dark brown to reddish-brown spots appear on the upper leaf surface. Spots may have yellowish margins. On the lower surface, dark fungal sporulation (olivaceous to black tufts) is visible under humid conditions.
- **Severe infections**: Spots coalesce, leading to large necrotic areas. Severely affected leaves may drop prematurely, exposing fruit to sunburn.
- **Fruit**: Not typically directly affected, but heavy defoliation reduces vine vigor and impacts fruit maturation and sugar accumulation.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in fallen infected leaves.
2. Conidia are produced on overwintered leaves in spring and dispersed by wind and rain.
3. Infection requires prolonged leaf wetness and warm temperatures (68-82°F / 20-28°C).
4. Secondary cycles occur throughout the growing season.
5. Disease severity increases in dense canopies with poor air circulation.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply copper-based fungicides preventively, beginning at bloom and continuing through veraison.
- Mancozeb is effective as a protectant fungicide.
- Strobilurin or triazole fungicides can also be used; rotate chemical classes to prevent resistance.

### Cultural Practices
- Remove and destroy infected leaves from the vineyard floor after leaf fall.
- Improve air circulation through vine training, shoot positioning, and selective leaf removal.
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization, which promotes dense canopy growth.
- Maintain open row spacing and manage cover crops to reduce humidity within the vine canopy.

### Organic Control
- Copper fungicides (Bordeaux mixture or copper hydroxide) are the primary organic option.
- Sulfur sprays provide some suppression.

## Prevention
- Choose cultivars with known tolerance when planting new vineyards.
- Maintain canopy management practices that promote air movement and rapid leaf drying.
- Remove fallen leaves in autumn to reduce overwintering inoculum.
