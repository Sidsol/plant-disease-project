# Tomato Septoria Leaf Spot (Septoria lycopersici)

## Overview
Septoria leaf spot is one of the most common and destructive foliar diseases of tomato, caused by the fungus *Septoria lycopersici*. It primarily attacks leaves and can cause severe defoliation, which exposes fruit to sunscald and reduces yield. The disease is particularly problematic in warm, wet climates.

## Symptoms
- **Leaves**: Numerous small (1-3 mm), circular spots with dark brown borders and light tan to gray centers. Tiny black dots (pycnidia — fungal fruiting bodies) are visible in the center of the spots under magnification. Lower leaves are affected first.
- **Progression**: Heavy infection causes leaves to yellow, wither, and drop. Defoliation progresses from the base of the plant upward.
- **Stems and Petioles**: Spots occasionally develop on stems and petioles but are less common.
- **Fruit**: Not directly infected, but defoliation leads to sunscald on exposed fruit.

## Disease Cycle
1. The fungus overwinters in infected tomato debris and on solanaceous weeds.
2. Water-borne conidia are splash-dispersed from overwintered debris to lower leaves.
3. Infection requires prolonged leaf wetness (12+ hours) and temperatures of 68-77°F (20-25°C).
4. Pycnidia form within lesions in 10-14 days and produce conidia for secondary spread.
5. Rain, overhead irrigation, and tools/hands spread spores through the canopy.
6. Multiple secondary cycles per season can lead to severe epidemics.

## Treatment

### Chemical Control
- Apply chlorothalonil at first sign of spots; repeat every 7-10 days during wet weather.
- Mancozeb, azoxystrobin, or difenoconazole are also effective.
- Alternate modes of action to manage fungicide resistance.
- Begin sprays at first symptom appearance or preventatively during wet weather.

### Cultural Practices
- Remove lower leaves showing symptoms — this slows upward spread.
- Avoid working in fields when foliage is wet (rain, dew, irrigation) to prevent spreading spores.
- Mulch around plants to prevent soil splash onto lower leaves.
- Stake or cage plants to keep foliage off the ground.
- Rotate with non-solanaceous crops for at least 2-3 years.
- Remove and destroy all tomato debris at end of season.

### Organic Control
- Copper-based fungicides provide moderate protection when applied preventatively.
- *Bacillus subtilis* (Serenade) sprays can reduce disease severity.

## Prevention
- Plant resistant varieties when available (limited, but some breeding lines show tolerance).
- Use drip irrigation and avoid overhead watering.
- Maintain adequate spacing for air circulation.
- Remove solanaceous weeds (nightshade, ground cherry) from around fields.
